# 6 — The PS-compliance slide

One slide, one table. Left column is the problem statement in its own words. Right column is
the screen that answers it and the number that screen prints for the demo case.

This is the slide to put up when a judge asks *"does it actually do what the PS asked?"* — and
the slide to leave up while you demo, because every row can be pointed at.

> **In plain words:** the government's problem statement, sentence by sentence down the left, and
> the thing in our product that answers it on the right. This is your proof slide — it exists to
> show you didn't build something adjacent to the brief.

**Everything in the right-hand column is a real output of `data/processed/cases/demo.json`,
generated by `.venv/bin/python scripts/run_api.py --build-demo` on scene `00223`.** Nothing
here is aspirational. If you change the pipeline, regenerate the case and re-read this file's
numbers off it — the commands are in §6.6.

> **Every number below is scene `00223`**, which is in the held-out test split and is what the
> picker labels `00223 · demo`. The old `00053` case — generated on 2026-09-04, before the retrain,
> by a superseded checkpoint, on a scene that is not in the current train/val/test split at all —

## 6.2 The two rows that win the slide

Do not let the table read as thirteen equal rows. Two of them are the ones other teams will not
have, and they are the two the PS was most prescriptive about.

**The filtering funnel.** Requirement (c) does not say "score the traffic" — it says
*"the irrelevant traffic is to be filtered out"*. That is an instruction to throw something
away **and be seen to throw it away**. Most submissions will show a ranked list of ten vessels
and call the low scores "filtered". Ours shows the population before the filter, the population
after, the rule, and the two distinct reasons a vessel can be excluded:

> 1,112 AIS reports · 10 vessels → 9 with reports in the release window → 2 intersect the origin
> envelope (within 3 envelope radii) → 8 excluded as irrelevant traffic

Say this out loud: **"the two exclusion reasons mean different things. Seven vessels were in the
window but 33 to 73 km away — those mean look at a different ship. One passed within 1.8 km, but
outside the window: right place, wrong time. That one isn't excluded by distance, it's excluded by
the width of our own release window, and tightening the window is a forcing-data problem."**

**The age we refused to fake.** Requirement (a) says *"and age if feasible"*. The tempting move
is to print the midpoint of the 24-hour window and call it 12 hours. We measured whether the
hindcast can tell one end of the window from the other, found it cannot, and say so — with the
arithmetic on screen.

> "The age is bounded at 24 hours, and we can show you why it can't be tightened: the estimated
> position moves 6.6 km while the uncertainty around it is 9.6 km. The whole window sits inside
> its own error bar. A second acquisition would fix it, and that is a procurement decision, not
> a modelling one."

To an NTRO audience — a technical intelligence organisation — a stated bound with its
derivation is worth more than a confident number. This row is the one that signals you
understand what you are allowed to claim.

## 6.3 The rows a judge will push on

Have the number ready. Do not improvise these.

| Push | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Your AIS is fake." | "Synthetic AIS is permitted in writing by the problem statement — *'Real AIS if available may be used else synthetic data can be prepared for the region of oil spill to demonstrate the functioning of the algorithm.'* The condition is that the **algorithm** must be real, and it is. And we emit the exact 17-column MarineCadastre schema the PS names as the format authority — you can download it from the app and diff it against a real daily extract. Switching to a live feed is a file drop, not a rewrite." |
| "The ocean forcing is synthetic too." | "Yes, and the app labels it on the card that reports it — the Drift screen's *Forcing* panel reads *Drift forcing: Synthetic scenario data*. The supplied CMEMS product doesn't cover this scene at this time — the interface prints the overlap test and the reason it failed rather than silently substituting. The physics is real: the field is the curl of a streamfunction, so it's analytically non-divergent, which we verify numerically in the test suite. If it weren't, the flow itself would concentrate the slick and every area number downstream would be an artefact of the integrator." |
| "0.584 IoU — how clean is that number?" | **Volunteer the split before they ask.** "Splits are grouped by parent Sentinel-1 product: 240 acquisitions, and **zero of them appear in two splits** — 169 train, 36 val, 35 test. We found a wiring bug where that grouping wasn't reaching the splitter, fixed it, added a regression test, and re-ran the whole thing; 0.584 pooled scene IoU is the post-fix number, not the leaky one. Patch scale is 0.769, but patches are sampled around labelled oil, so quote the scene figure. Both are measured against the dataset's own masks, whose own accuracy we can't audit." |
| "Can it reject a look-alike — an algal bloom, a low-wind glassy patch?" | "Partly, and here's the number. The supplied dataset has no labelled look-alikes, so we scored ourselves against 2,290 published ones from an independent archive — a different sea, nothing our model trained on. On a stratified 340-patch sample the U-Net **on its own raises an alarm on 288 of them**, which tells you a dark patch is not evidence of oil. So there's a screen in front of it: seven shape-and-contrast features per dark region, which rejects **69%** of the 84,758 dark regions while keeping 90% of real oil at AUC 0.957 held out. Real improvement, not a solution — a third of those regions still survive. It's in `lookalike_metrics.json` with seven stated limitations." |
| "Which vessel did it?" | "We don't answer that, and that's deliberate. The strongest phrase anywhere in the product is **'Priority candidate for investigation.'** We rank by spatio-temporal correlation with every component and its evidence exposed. A false accusation against a named, identifiable vessel is a diplomatic problem, not a software bug." |
| "Why foreign data and not Indian waters?" | **Correct the premise first.** "The dataset is global, not regional: 1,200 scenes across 24 seas, 95°W to 130°E. Our demo scene happens to be Central Mediterranean; the largest block is the Gulf of Mexico at 388 scenes, and the Persian Gulf is 88 of them, about 7%. And it's the dataset the problem statement itself points to — Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.8346860, published with a Marine Pollution Bulletin paper. Where you're right: **there is not one scene in Indian water**, and the pipeline is region-agnostic only in the sense that it reads each scene's own corner coordinates. Adding a Gulf of Kutch or Ennore scene from the Copernicus Data Space is on the roadmap as a generalisation test, not a fix." |

## 6.4 How to build the actual slide

Keep it to **one** slide. Thirteen rows will not fit legibly on a projector, so:

- **Print the full table as a handout** (this file, §6.1) and put it on the judges' table.
- **On screen, show five rows:** (a) detect, (a) age, (b) origin point *and* time,
  (c) filtering funnel, (c) scoring. Those five are the spine of the PS.
- Set the left column at ~35 % width, italic, in the PS's own words with the key phrase in
  bold. Set the right column at ~65 %, with **the number in bold and the screen name first**.
- Every right-hand cell must name a screen. The point of the slide is that each claim is one
  click away — and during the demo you should actually make that click.
- No logos, no icons, no gradient. A judge reading a compliance table wants density, not
  design.

## 6.5 The one-paragraph version

If you get thirty seconds instead of a slide:

> The statement has three requirements. **(a)** We segment the slick and characterise it — 12
> regions, 26.9 km², pooled scene IoU 0.584 against a 0.676 classical baseline at patch scale
> where the like-for-like comparison is 0.769 — and we bound its age at 24 hours, with the
> arithmetic showing why one acquisition can't tighten that. **(b)** We
> hindcast to an origin point *and* time — 14.52 °E, 35.89 °N, ±9.6 km, in a 24-hour release
> window — under both currents and wind, then forecast forward. **(c)** We reconstruct AIS traffic
> through that window in the exact MarineCadastre schema, filter it — 10 vessels down to 2, with
> the 8 exclusions retained and reasoned so the filter can be audited — and score what's left on
> proximity, trajectory and behavioural anomaly, every component showing the sentence that
> earned it. All ten stages run in 20 seconds from one command, and the interface has zero
> dependencies. The AIS and the ocean forcing are synthetic, each labelled as such on the card
> that reports it, and permitted in writing by the statement.

## 6.6 Regenerating the numbers in this file

Every figure above comes from the stored demo case or the metrics file. After any pipeline
change, rebuild and re-read them:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/run_api.py --build-demo && .venv/bin/python scripts/build_web.py
```

Then print the rows this slide depends on:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -c "import json; d=json.load(open('data/processed/cases/demo.json')); s=d['slick']; a=d['spillAge']; f=d['attribution']['filtering']; o=d['drift']['backward']['originEstimate']; print('slick   ', s['slickCount'], 'regions', s['totalAreaKm2'], 'km2 total,', s['areaKm2'], 'km2 largest'); print('age     ', a['label'], '| resolvable:', a['resolution']['resolvable'], '| ratio', a['resolution']['bestSeparation']['ratio']); print('origin  ', o['centroid'], 'P50', o['radiusP50Km'], 'P90', o['radiusP90Km']); print('funnel  ', f['summary']); print('top     ', d['attribution']['candidates'][0]['score'], '/', d['attribution']['candidates'][0]['scoreMax']); print('timing  ', d['timing']['totalSeconds'], 's total')"
```

And the accuracy figures, which come from the model evaluation rather than the case:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -c "import json; m=json.load(open('data/processed/scene_metrics.json')); t=m['test']['atSceneThreshold']; print('scene IoU pooled', round(t['pooled']['iou'],4), '| precision', round(t['pooled']['precision'],4), '| recall', round(t['pooled']['recall'],4), '| scenes', t['scenes'])"
```
